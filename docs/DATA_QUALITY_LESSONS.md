# Data Quality Lessons from the Pre-Phase-1 Signals Dataset

The project's original historical signals dataset, the `signals_*.parquet` files generated by `scripts/generate_signals.py` before the forward indexer was built, had several data-quality problems severe enough that no conclusion drawn from backtesting against that dataset was meaningful. This document records those problems so future work does not repeat them and so the current paper-only stance is grounded in a concrete record of why the prior results were rejected, not only in rubric thresholds.

## Problem 1: Lookahead fallback contamination

The original signal generation needed historical price snapshots at signal time. When the historical CLOB price endpoint had no data for a given timestamp, the pipeline fell back to using the resolved price as the historical price. This was marked via the `is_lookahead` boolean column, and the deep backtester filtered those rows out.

The most recent expanded signal file still required this fallback on 12.69% of rows. That is not a small data gap. It means one in eight signals used future information as an input.

This invalidated results for three reasons:

- Even with lookahead rows filtered out at backtest time, feature distributions in the remaining 87% were shaped by which markets had clean historical data, not by which markets were representative of the strategy universe.
- Markets with thin historical pricing are exactly the markets where the strategy has the most uncertainty about fair value. Filtering them out preferentially kept easier signals and silently improved apparent performance.
- The fix is not better lookahead detection. The fix is signal production only when real historical data exists. The forward indexer exists to make that possible going forward.

## Problem 2: Aggregated price candles, not order book snapshots

The historical Polymarket data used by the signals pipeline came from aggregated CLOB price history. It was candle-like price history, not order book state.

This invalidated results for three reasons:

- A liquidity-harvest strategy depends on book depth at the moment of signal, not summary candle prices. Candle data cannot determine whether a maker quote would have been filled, at what price, or against what kind of counterparty.
- The 20% adverse-selection haircut applied in the liquidity-harvest backtester was a plug, not a measurement. Without real book history, the system could not compute actual adverse-selection rate against historical resting quotes.
- Strategy decisions made on candle-derived signals say nothing about whether the strategy executes profitably on real order books. The forward indexer captures book snapshots at 15-second cadence specifically to make this measurable.

## Problem 3: Activity filter on noisy fields, not real liquidity

The original activity filter used `volume_24h OR spread < threshold` with permissive thresholds: `$1,000` volume or `$0.20` spread. After the forward data-layer tightening changed this to AND logic with `$10,000` volume, `$0.10` spread, recent activity, minimum market age, and minimum time to close, the tracked-market count on Polymarket dropped from 31,004 to about 500. That is a 60x reduction.

This invalidated results for three reasons:

- The 31,004 markets that passed the original filter included many with calculated or stale spreads on markets with no real trading. These markets contributed features that looked numerically valid but did not reflect tradeable conditions.
- Including more than 30,000 inactive markets in feature generation diluted the signal from the few hundred markets that were actually liquid. The model learned patterns from noise.
- Activity filter logic is the difference between a research universe of markets that exist and a tradeable universe of markets where the strategy can plausibly operate. Conflating the two systematically biased every downstream metric.

## Problem 4: LLM-derived features with unverifiable temporal contamination

The signals pipeline includes LLM-derived sentiment, news, and probability features. These were generated using local Ollama models such as `qwen2.5:32b` and `llama3.2`, which is the correct mitigation against frontier-model contamination.

Even local models with known cutoffs cannot guarantee zero leakage. News articles processed at signal time were dated, but the model's training data can include articles or analyses that retrospectively reference the resolution of the event being signaled.

This invalidated results for two reasons:

- LLM features were positive contributors to model probabilities in some categories. The existing dataset cannot determine whether that contribution came from genuine extractable signal or from training data overlapping with resolution information.
- The fix is not removal of LLM features. The fix is validation against a holdout where the LLM training cutoff strictly predates the signal generation date and the resolution date. The original dataset cannot be re-cut to guarantee that condition.

## Problem 5: Resolution treated as a clean signal

The pipeline treated market resolution as a deterministic, error-free event. In reality:

- Polymarket has UMA dispute processes that can change resolutions days after initial settlement.
- Kalshi has clarification rulings that can change outcomes for ambiguous markets.
- Some markets resolve in ways that do not match the apparent question, including changed resolution sources or platform-specific interpretation of definitions.

This invalidated results for two reasons:

- A strategy that wins on the apparent meaning of the question but loses on the platform's official resolution earns zero.
- The pipeline did not capture this distinction. The forward schema includes `Resolution`, and the planned resolution-watcher process exists to capture resolution events as discrete observations, including dispute or clarification activity, so future backtests can model resolution risk explicitly.

## Problem 6: Sample size masquerading as statistical power

The most recent signal file contained 47,932 rows across 1,575 crypto markets. The deep backtester reported confidence intervals on this sample.

This invalidated results for three reasons:

- The unit of statistical inference for a probability-betting strategy is resolved markets, not signal rows.
- A single market generates many signal rows over its lifetime but produces only one resolved outcome. The 1,575-market sample is much smaller than 47,932 rows implies.
- With non-uniform category coverage concentrated in a few popular event types, the effective independent-event count is smaller still. Row-level confidence intervals on a strategy whose actual decisions are made per market are statistically meaningless.

## What proper data means in this project

The current path to trustworthy validation is concrete:

1. Forward indexer captures real book state, not derived candles. Phase 1 is deployed.
2. Kalshi WebSocket authentication raises Kalshi data quality to parity with Polymarket. This is deferred to Phase 1.5.
3. Activity filtering runs at source on real, current liquidity signals. Phase 1 is deployed.
4. Resolution-watcher process captures resolution events, including dispute and clarification activity. This is not yet built.
5. Execution simulator consumes captured book state to produce realistic fill estimates against historical resting quotes. This is not yet built.
6. Strategy decisions are evaluated on per-market resolution counts, not per-row signal counts, with pre-registered Go criteria. This waits on sufficient forward data accumulation.

Until those pieces are in place, backtest results are research artifacts only, regardless of how favorable the headline numbers look.
